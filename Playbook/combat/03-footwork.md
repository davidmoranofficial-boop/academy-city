# Footwork and stance

## Stance
- Feet about shoulder width, lead foot forward, back heel slightly raised; feet never cross (crossing = off balance).
- Width you can step from in any direction without first bringing the feet together. Wide = stable but slow (Shell/defensive styles); narrow = fast but less stable (Runner/agility styles). **Use stance width to show style and stance switching.**
- Knees soft, weight about 50/50 (Muay Thai: more weight on the back foot so the lead leg can check).
- Guard: rear hand at the cheek/chin, lead hand forward at eye height, elbows in.

## Moving
- **Step-drag**: the foot nearest the direction of travel moves first, the other follows the same distance, restoring the stance. Forward = lead foot first. Back = back foot first. Sideways = the foot on that side first.
- Short steps, feet barely leave the ground. Never walk (crossing feet, heel-to-toe walking cycle) inside fighting range.
- **Pivot**: turn on the ball of the lead foot to change angle; the back foot swings round.
- **Circling**: small steps + pivots, always facing the opponent.
- Distance management: a fighter steps in to strike and steps out after, so the gap "breathes". Constant static distance looks robotic.

## What David asked for (8 Nov)
- Actual steps, not sliding. Back steps and side steps.
- Simultaneous movement: both fighters adjust at once.
- Steps matched to the distance covered (no moonwalking).

## Mistakes to watch for
- Feet sliding while the body moves (see 06 checks).
- A walking cycle used for 1-2 tile adjustments.
- Turning on the spot without stepping the feet.
- Fighters drifting out of facing while moving.
