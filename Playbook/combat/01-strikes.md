# Strikes: how they should look

Core rule for every strike: **power comes from the ground up**. Feet push, hips turn, shoulders follow, the limb is last. If only the arm or leg moves, it looks weak and fake.

Every strike has five phases. All five must be visible:
1. **Face**: attacker is square-ish to the target (within ~30 degrees) before anything starts.
2. **Step / load**: small step to range, weight shifts, slight dip or chamber (the anticipation). This is the "tell" Vision reads.
3. **Strike (active)**: fast. Fewer frames than the wind-up. Limb reaches the target zone exactly at the contact frame.
4. **Contact**: hit stop on both fighters (see 04-impact).
5. **Recover**: limb retracts on the same path, hands back to guard, stance width restored. Slower than the strike.

Timing guide (fighting-game practice, Skullgirls GDC talk): anticipation is readable, the strike itself is very short (a few frames), recovery is longer than the strike. A fast character shortens anticipation and recovery, not just the strike.

## Punches

| Punch | Looks right | Common mistakes |
|---|---|---|
| Jab (lead hand) | Small weight shift onto the front foot, shoulder rolls up to cover the chin, fist turns over at full extension, snaps back | Whole body lunging; elbow flaring out; rear hand dropping |
| Cross (rear hand) | Rear heel lifts and pivots (toe points at target), hips and shoulders rotate fully, weight goes from back foot to front foot, lead hand guards the chin | No rear-foot pivot (= arm punch); leaning past the front knee |
| Hook | Elbow bent ~90 degrees, arm a rigid frame, power from turning the lead foot and hip; weight shifts the opposite way to the cross | Arm swinging wide like a slap; feet not turning |
| Body shot | Knees bend to drop level (do not bend at the waist), punch travels slightly upward into the ribs | Bending forward at the waist with straight legs |

Elite vs novice (Notre Dame biomechanics note): elite boxers use much more hip rotation on hooks and uppercuts; novices use the shoulder. **If the hips don't turn, it reads as a novice.** Useful for showing skill gaps between characters.

## Kicks

### Low kick (Muay Thai style), the one David flagged
Order matters. One leg kicks, the other is planted the whole time.
1. Small step out and forward with the **lead** foot, angled outward (opens the hips).
2. Pivot on the **ball of the standing foot**, heel turns toward the target, knee slightly bent.
3. Hips and shoulders turn together; the arm on the kicking side swings down/back for balance; the other hand guards the face.
4. Kicking leg swings like a bat. **Shin** (lower-middle of the shin), not the foot, lands on the **thigh just above the knee** (outer thigh, or inner thigh for an inside kick). Slight downward cut.
5. Leg returns to stance, or steps through into a southpaw stance if it missed.

Mistakes: kicking with the foot; standing foot flat and not pivoting (no power); standing leg lifting off; both legs moving at once; leaning backward away from the kick (our free-pack kick does this); target too low (shin to shin looks like a trip).

### Body kick / roundhouse
Same as the low kick, but the hip turns over more (the kicking hip ends up facing the floor/forward) and the shin lands on the ribs/liver. A slight lean back is normal for a high kick, NOT for a body kick. The standing heel turns to face the target.

### Head kick
Hip fully turned over, upper body leans back to counterbalance, the shin or top of the foot lands on the head or neck. This is a big committal move: long tell, long recovery.

### Teep (push kick)
Knee lifts to chamber, then the ball of the foot pushes straight into the stomach. It moves the opponent back rather than hurting much. Useful for spacing and for stopping a charge.

## Readable targeting (David: "not obvious where each player is aiming")
- The attacker's eyes and head point at the target zone during the load phase.
- Head strikes: fist or shin travels at head height from the first strike frame. Body: the attacker dips. Legs: the attacker's hips turn early and the shoulder drops.
- Optional UI: a brief highlight on the targeted body area on the human outline (step 6).
